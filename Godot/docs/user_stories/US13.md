# US13: Train and deploy through a production example

- **Owner:** Bohdan
- **Status:** RaceTrack rebuilt with hills, jumps, obstacles and a ray sensor, and retrained, on feature branch
- **Priority:** MVP (highest priority)
- **Last updated:** Nov. 21, 2026

## Story

As a new Schola user, I want a complete Godot example that trains through the production integration so that I can verify my setup and use the example as a starting point for my own environment.

## Rationale

The project specification requires an example environment in which an agent successfully learns. Earlier course demos are frozen historical artifacts and may contain temporary stand-ins, so the production workflow needs a new example that exercises the completed Godot integration end to end.

## Acceptance criteria

- The example uses the production environment, agent, lifecycle, and gRPC implementations without local transport or lifecycle stand-ins.
- Training runs through at least one Python framework already supported by Schola.
- A documented metric demonstrates reproducible learning beyond an untrained baseline.
- The trained policy can be exported to ONNX.
- Godot loads the exported policy and runs the learned behavior with Python closed.
- The example uses production sensor, actuator, space, and policy APIs where applicable.
- Setup, training, export, inference, and common failure recovery are documented from a clean checkout.
- Automated smoke tests verify that the example loads and that its production components are configured correctly.
- The example is created in a new current location; frozen D1 and D2 demos remain unchanged.

## Progress

The RaceTrack example is on `us13-production-example` ([PR #50](https://github.com/csc301-2026-f/20-AMD-Schola/pull/50)) under `Godot/examples/race_track/`; see its [README](../../examples/race_track/README.md). The branch includes `main` through the US3 scene connector ([PR #59](https://github.com/csc301-2026-f/20-AMD-Schola/pull/59), [`ba4ea72`](https://github.com/csc301-2026-f/20-AMD-Schola/commit/ba4ea72)), and its training scene uses that connector.

### Metric

The track was rebuilt on Nov. 20 with hills, jumps and obstacles, and the car gained a ray sensor. The 650,000-step policy was trained from scratch for the rebuilt track with `train.py` for 350,000 steps, then resumed for 300,000 more after obstacle contact began ending episodes and the drift bonus was raised. The committed policy uses a bigger network (two hidden layers of 256 units instead of 64) that reads all 25 rays and the RoadAhead sensor.

`demo_check.gd` drives the inference scene at a fixed 60 Hz without Python: two laps of the demo map, 30 obstacles dropped 50 m ahead of the car, and 10 random obstacle layouts. A policy passes with a clean demo map at 50 km/h or more, at least 27 dodges and at least 8 layouts. Repeated runs give the same result.

| Key | Policy | Demo map | Average speed | Drifting | Dodged | Layouts | Result |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `policies/race_track_untrained.onnx` | 61 m, no lap | 2 km/h | 0 s | 0 of 30 | 0 of 10 | Fail |
| 2 | `policies/race_track_350k_steps.onnx` | 2 laps, 20 contacts | 78 km/h | 0 s | 16 of 30 | 0 of 10 | Fail |
| 3 | `policies/race_track_650k_steps.onnx` | 2 laps, no contact | 81 km/h | 18 s | 18 of 30 | 0 of 10 | Fail |
| 4 | `policies/race_track.onnx` (committed) | 2 laps, no contact | 58 km/h | 69 s | 30 of 30 | 10 of 10 | Pass |

## Scope limits

- This production example is required for D4, as confirmed at the Nov. 6 partner meeting.
- One well-documented benchmark environment is sufficient.
- Supporting every Python training framework in the example is not required; the engine protocol must remain compatible with the shared Schola Python stack.
- This story validates existing production components rather than creating a second implementation for the example.
